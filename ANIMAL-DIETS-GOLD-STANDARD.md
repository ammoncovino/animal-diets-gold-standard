# ANIMAL DIETS — GOLD STANDARD OPERATING PLAN

**Project:** LIFE Animal Diet Governance System  
**Purpose:** A single, clean, GitHub-ready source of truth for animal feeding standards, browse use, food preparation, enrichment boundaries, and staff execution.

> **Scope:** Healthy animals on standard husbandry diets. Sick, neonatal, pregnant/lactating, underweight, recovering, or special-care animals may require veterinarian-directed modifications.

# 1. CORE OPERATING PRINCIPLES

## Match the diet — do not improvise
- Staff do not invent diets.
- Staff do not substitute ingredients without approval.
- Staff do not add leftovers from another species.
- Staff do not turn one exhibit's diet into another exhibit's diet.
- If an item is not on the approved diet, it does not go into the bowl.
- If an animal repeatedly refuses an approved item, record that and escalate for review rather than silently replacing it.

## Feed the species, not the leftovers
The fact that an animal **will eat** an item does not mean that item belongs in its standard diet.

## Structure matters
- Grazers: hay / grass dominant
- Folivores: leaves / browse dominant
- Frugivores: controlled fruit with fiber/browse appropriate to species
- Carnivores: animal protein dominant
- Insectivores: insect-based feeding dominant
- Mixed primates: browse/fiber, controlled fruit, and nutritional anchor

## Use the least processing necessary
- Whole leaves when practical
- Large natural pieces instead of finely chopped salads
- No blending simply for convenience
- No mash or puree unless medically or behaviorally justified
- Cutting is for safety, portioning, medication, or demonstrated intake benefit — not habit

## Observe actual intake
Track what is eaten first, what is left behind, amount offered, amount remaining, body condition, stool quality, social competition, and maternal/breeding status.

# 3. CAPYBARA

**Species:** *Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris*  
**Feeding type:** Grass grazer / hindgut fermenter

## CORRECT
Primary:
- Coastal hay
- Timothy hay
- Grass hay
- Fresh-cut grass
- Safe pasture grass

Small supplemental greens:
- Collard greens
- Kale
- Cabbage in small amounts

## WRONG
Do not build the diet around:
- vegetable bowls
- carrots
- zucchini
- squash
- corn
- fruit
- large amounts of cabbage or lettuce replacing forage

## SIMPLE BUILD
- 90–95% grass / hay / pasture
- 5–10% supplemental leafy greens

## PREP RULE
Hay and grass remain whole and freely available. Greens can be whole or in large pieces. Do not create chopped salad bowls.

## 3-SECOND CHECK
**Do I see grass or hay as the main food?**

## HARD RULE
**Hay and grass are the diet. Produce is supplemental.**

## WRONG

## 3-SECOND CHECK

# 5. PATAGONIAN MARA / PATAGONIAN CAVY

**Species:** *Dolichotis patagonum*

## CORRECT
Primary:
- Unlimited grass hay
- Timothy hay
- Coastal hay
- Pasture grass
- Fresh-cut grass

Support:
- Safe forage
- Dandelion greens
- Clover
- Small leafy greens

For confirmed under-condition:
- Controlled supplemental alfalfa hay
- Measured appropriate herbivore pellet only when body-condition management or forage quality requires it
- Monitor body weight and body condition rather than using fruit for weight gain

## WRONG
- Fruit-heavy feeding
- Carrots as routine food
- Bell peppers as routine food
- Zucchini or squash bowls
- Sweet potato
- Mixed vegetable bowls
- Replacing hay with pellets

## HARD RULE
**Pellets are nutritional insurance or a body-condition tool, not the biological base diet.**

# 6. WALLABY / MACROPOD

## CORRECT
- Grass hay
- Timothy hay
- Coastal hay
- Fresh pasture / fresh-cut grass where safe
- Species-appropriate macropod pellet only as a measured supplement where needed

## WRONG
- Pellet-dominant feeding
- Fruit-heavy feeding
- Large amounts of carrots, apples, bananas, or mixed vegetables
- Using encounter food as the core nutritional diet

## HARD RULE
**Macropod pellets support the forage diet; they do not replace it.**

# 7. ALPACA

## CORRECT

- Timothy or appropriate grass hay
- Mineral supplementation as professionally directed

## WRONG

# 8. TWO-TOED SLOTH

**Genus:** *Choloepus*

## CORRECT
Nutritional anchor:
- Approved sloth / leaf-eater / folivore biscuit or chow

Leaf component:
- Collard greens
- Dandelion greens
- Mustard greens
- Mulberry leaves if actually consumed
- Hibiscus leaves if verified safe and consumed
- Other approved browse

## BISCUIT PREPARATION
- Whole or lightly softened is acceptable depending on individual preference.
- If softened, use only enough water to soften.
- Do not turn into soup, slurry, or blender puree unless medically required.

## VEGETABLES
Sloths can physically eat some vegetables, but vegetables should not displace chow, browse, or fibrous leaves.

Lower-priority items:
- zucchini
- squash
- cucumber
- green beans
- bell pepper

If repeatedly left uneaten, remove them from routine use.

## WRONG
- Vegetable salad as the main diet
- Romaine-heavy feeding
- No chow
- Cross-exhibit leftovers
- Blended produce as routine feeding

## 3-SECOND CHECK
**Do I see approved chow plus real leaves/browse?**

# 11. INFANT SPIDER MONKEY — SPECIAL CARE NOTE

For a 10-week-old infant:
- Formula remains the primary nutritional concern.
- Do not replace formula with human protein shakes such as Fairlife Core Power.
- Human protein shakes are not formulated as infant primate milk replacers.
- Track body weight, 24-hour intake, hydration, nipple flow, formula temperature, feeding frequency, and illness signs.
- Veterinary / experienced primate neonatal oversight is required for persistent refusal or poor growth.

# 14. RING-TAILED LEMUR — CURRENT GOLD STANDARD

**Species:** *Lemur catta*

## WHAT THIS ANIMAL IS
A mixed primate / seasonal generalist, not an extreme frugivore like ruffed lemurs.

## CORRECT HIERARCHY

### PRIMARY
Browse + fibrous greens:
- Collard greens
- Mustard greens
- Dandelion greens
- Mulberry
- Hibiscus
- Other verified safe primate browse

### SECONDARY
Controlled fruit:
- Papaya
- Mango
- Cantaloupe
- Other melon
- Berries

Limit:
- Banana
- Grapes

### SUPPORT
Approved low-iron primate biscuit / chow, used as a nutritional anchor rather than the visual bulk of the diet.

## CANTALOUPE
Acceptable as part of the fruit rotation. It should not dominate the overall diet.

## TAMARIND
Can be tested in small controlled amounts. Do not make it the dominant fruit item.

## MULBERRY ACCEPTANCE
If mulberry leaves are repeatedly refused:
- test freshness
- test whole-branch presentation
- test lightly chopped presentation
- test other browse
- record actual intake

## USING SWEET FOODS TO INTRODUCE BROWSE
A preferred food can be used as a training bridge, but not as a permanent sugar-delivery system.

Preferred:
- small mango pieces
- small papaya pieces

Use more cautiously:
- grapes
- banana

Gradually reduce sweet attractant and confirm browse remains accepted.

## WRONG
- carrots
- sweet potato
- oranges / citrus
- bell pepper
- zucchini
- squash
- romaine
- random leftovers from other exhibits

## PREP RULE
- Fruit in chunks
- Leaves whole or large
- Browse natural or coarsely processed for a defined trial
- No blender puree as routine feeding

## 3-SECOND CHECK
**Do I see browse/fiber first, controlled fruit second, and nutritional support third?**

## HARD RULE
**Ringtail diets are not ruffed-lemur diets. Keep fruit controlled.**

# 23. BENGAL CAT

## CORRECT

# 27. BROWSE GOVERNANCE

Current browse resources:
- Mulberry
- Hibiscus
- Acacia
- Willow
- Grape vine
- Local grasses

## RULE
Not all browse is universal.

### Acacia
Do not automatically give acacia to every species. Use only where verified safe and actually consumed.

### Giraffe browse
Acacia and willow can be tested separately for preference.

### If browse is not eaten
- Observe
- Record
- Remove when stale
- Rotate to another approved browse
- Do not keep filling an enclosure with a browse the animals consistently ignore

# 28. DRIED LEAF / BROWSE TRIAL

Food dryers may be used experimentally.

## TEST BATCHES
Batch A:
- 100% dried mulberry

Batch B:
- 100% dried hibiscus

Batch C:
- 75% mulberry
- 25% hibiscus

Process to coarse flakes, not fine powder.

If a binder / attractant is needed:
- small mashed mango
- small mashed papaya

## RULE
The goal is to determine whether the animal accepts the browse, not whether sugar can hide it.

# 29. FRUIT AS A TRAINING BRIDGE

Preferred:
- Mango
- Papaya

Use cautiously:
- Grapes
- Banana

Use tiny amounts, reduce over time, and monitor whether acceptance remains.

# 30. FOOD PROCESSING RULES

## GOOD REASONS TO CUT
- Choking prevention
- Portion control
- Medication
- Small-bird accessibility
- Defined behavioral or intake purpose

## POOR REASONS TO CUT
- Habit
- Convenience
- Appearance
- "We always do it"

## BLENDER RULE
Routine blending of fruit for primates is discouraged. Prefer whole or chunked approved foods.

# 31. FROZEN FRUIT ENRICHMENT

Preferred:
- whole frozen fruit pieces
- browse bundles
- hidden fruit in leaves
- scatter feeds
- puzzle feeders

Avoid as routine enrichment:
- fruit juice ice cubes
- blended fruit cubes
- sweet liquid enrichment

# 32. EARLY-MORNING FEEDING

## Lemurs
- Strong early-morning anticipation may be observed.
- Feed consistently and early where welfare and social dynamics support it.
- Use multiple feeding points when competition is a concern.
- Protect maternal access.

## Otters
Multiple daily feedings are common; do not turn "every 3 hours" into a universal rule without species-specific confirmation.

## Penguins
Multiple fish feedings are common, but not all species require a strict every-three-hours schedule.

# 33. MATERNAL / TROOP FEEDING

For lemur troops with infants:
- use multiple feeding stations
- reduce monopolization
- avoid forcing mothers to defend infants near a single high-value bowl
- distribute preferred items
- ensure lactating females can access food without social pressure

# 37. CROSS-EXHIBIT DRIFT TO PREVENT

Examples:
- Lemur fruit into parakeet bowls
- Carrots into ringtail diets
- Acacia into every exhibit
- Pellets becoming the primary diet for grazers
- Fruit becoming the primary diet for ringtails
- Grocery vegetables replacing browse

# 38. FINAL SYSTEM PRINCIPLES

1. Understand the animal before feeding the animal.
2. The approved species diet outranks convenience.
3. What an animal will eat is not the same as what should form its routine diet.
4. Observe actual consumption.
5. Use browse and forage intelligently, not universally.
6. Keep grazers grazing.
7. Keep folivores on leaves/browse plus appropriate nutritional support.
8. Keep ringtails distinct from ruffed lemurs.
9. Use fruit strategically, not indiscriminately.
10. Avoid turning feeding into a leftover-disposal system.
11. Do not replace infant formula with human nutrition products.
12. Special-care animals require individualized review.
13. Match the diet. Do not improvise.
